How it works

01

First half ends

The model reads goals, shots, possession and ~45 other 1st-half metrics the moment they're played.

02

Compares it against history

It checks them against more than 3,900 Bundesliga first halves from 2013 to 2024.

03

You get the pick

Before the second half kicks off, you already know how likely it is to have over 1.5 goals.

Wie das Modell funktioniert

The target, the algorithm, the ~30 input variables, and how the model in production was chosen and measured.
Model target
Over 1.5 goals in the 2nd half?
Binary classification (Over/Under 1.5) trained only on 1st-half data — nothing from the 2nd half enters the model, so the prediction is useful right at halftime.

The pipeline, end to end

01

EDA

Chronological Train/Test split, univariate/bivariate analysis, missingness and correlations across ~45 metrics per match half.

02

Feature Engineering

9 families of features derived from the 1st half (see below), with two independent preprocessing tracks: one for linear models, one for trees.

03

Training

8 algorithm families tuned with temporal cross-validation (expanding-window TimeSeriesSplit), refitting the preprocessing inside every fold to avoid data leakage.

04

Evaluation

Winner selection, probability calibration, threshold choice, and final evaluation on Test — touched exactly once.

The 9 variable families (1st half)

Everything is computed exclusively from what happened in the first half.
Differences & totals
xG, shots, corners, fouls: home–away difference and sum.
Efficiency
Pass accuracy, goal conversion per shot on target.
Shot accuracy
Shots on target / total shots, by team and difference.
Halftime score
Goal difference and total, draw state, pressure, who's ahead at the break.
Numerical superiority
1st-half red cards and their effect on numerical advantage.
Market (odds)
Implied 1X2 probabilities and the bookmaker's margin.
Market × score interaction
How the market's expectation shifts with the halftime score.
Temporal
Days of rest for each team since its previous match.
Recent form
Each team's 2nd-half goal history in previous matches (stateful).
→ See the full breakdown of all 30 variables, per match, on the Model page.

How the winning model was chosen

01 · Training

8 families tuned (logistic regression, SVM, KNN, random forest, extra trees, gradient boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM) via Optuna/grid search, using the one-standard-error rule to pick each family's winning configuration.

02 · Selection

The 8 candidates are ranked by their cross-validation score. The winner is chosen via paired bootstrap (rank 1 vs. rank 2) on the ROC-AUC difference, tie-breaking toward the simpler model.

03 · Calibration

The model picks between sigmoid and isotonic calibration (whichever minimizes the Brier score) via nested cross-fitting, then refits on 100% of the predictions.

04 · Threshold

The cutoff is chosen between a fixed 0.5, max F1, or whichever maximizes expected value betting against the market's odds (Over/Under 1.5).
